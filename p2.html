Neighborly

Neighborly Native App

Neighborly Native App: One Framework, Shipped Twice

My role

Led UX strategy, IA, research, and booking flow design

Result

The OPUS jobs framework (Repair / Maintain / Enhance) became the app’s primary navigation and core booking flow

Timeline

5 Months



The problem

The app was live but fell short for both sides of the marketplace. Customers couldn’t easily browse or understand 20+ service brands, and franchise owners weren’t getting the booking volume or lead quality the platform promised. The relaunch was scoped to fix that with clearer brand descriptions, offers by zip code, a real booking flow instead of a lead form, and live job status.

Decision 1: Reuse the strategy instead of redesigning from scratch


The app’s home screen is a direct application of the framework validated on the web platform. The mental model that organized a 20-brand website migration is what a customer taps on day one.

Decision 2: Benchmark beyond the category


I audited direct competitors (Urban Company, Pinch, JOBOY) and adjacent apps with high-trust booking flows (Marriott Bonvoy, Airbnb, Hotels.com, Expedia, TripAdvisor). Home services have more in common with booking a stay than with e-commerce checkout: infrequent, higher stakes, tied to a time and place. That shaped the multi-step booking flow and the “upcoming jobs” screen.

Decision 3: Add friction where it prevents bad leads


The flow runs Home → Brand Selection → Brand Details → Service Type → Booking. I added a lightweight service type bumper between brand details and booking, because a single brand (Five Star Painting, for example) offers service types with different booking requirements, and skipping that step was sending franchise owners mismatched leads.

Decision 4: Design one flow for two mindsets

The booking flow itself, service frequency, schedule selection, contact details, review, confirmation, was designed around the same two personas from OPUS:

  • Diane (Adapter) - Diane gets visibility and control: a step indicator, a review step, and exact preference inputs.

  • Elizabeth (Optimizer) - Elizabeth gets speed: guest checkout, sensible defaults, and a “call us directly” option on every screen.

Decision 5: Bring people back without making them remember

Offers surface by zip code and brand. A persistent “upcoming jobs” view consolidates every scheduled service across every brand the customer has used, with a fallback to add a phone or job number for call-center bookings.
